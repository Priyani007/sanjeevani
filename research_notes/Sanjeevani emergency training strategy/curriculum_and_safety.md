# Sanjeevani Emergency Training Curriculum and Medical Safety

## AHA 2020 / ERC 2021 / ILCOR Guidelines for Adult BLS/CPR

### Takeaway
Adult BLS guidelines from AHA 2020, ERC 2021, and ILCOR 2020 are tightly aligned on the core sequence and metrics; the training app must teach compressions at 100-120/min, depth 5-6 cm, ratio 30:2, and immediate AED use as deterministic non-negotiable steps.

### Cited Findings
- AHA 2020 adult compression rate: 100-120 per minute. Compression depth: at least 2 inches (5 cm), not more than 2.4 inches (6 cm). Ratio: 30:2. — [AHA 2020 Guidelines for CPR and ECC](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2020-aha)
- AHA BLS sequence: recognize unresponsiveness, check breathing, activate emergency services (dial 112 in India), begin compressions, add rescue breaths if trained, use AED as soon as available. — [AHA BLS Guidelines](https://cpr.heart.org/en/test-cpr-pages/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines-2020/adult-basic-and-advanced-life-support)
- ERC 2021 adult BLS: compressions at 100-120/min, depth 5-6 cm, 30:2 ratio for adults. Emphasis on early defibrillation. Guidance updated to include COVID-19 precautions. — [ERC 2021 Executive Summary](https://www.erc.edu/assets/documents/RESUS-8995-Exec-Summary_copy.pdf)
- Over 400,000 people suffer cardiac arrest each year in Europe; AEDs are used in only 28% of cases (range 4-59%). — [ERC 2021 Guidelines via BORIS Unibe](https://boris.unibe.ch/156467)
- ILCOR 2020 CoSTR addressed 184 topics across six task forces: BLS, ALS, Pediatric, Neonatal, Education/Implementation/Teams, and First Aid. Only systematic reviews with GRADE analysis could generate new treatment recommendations. — [ILCOR 2020 via AHA](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2020-international-consensus-on-cardiopulmonary-resuscitation-science-with-treatment-recommendations)
- ILCOR 2020 OHCA US survival rates: average 10% out-of-hospital, 27% in-hospital for adults. — [ILCOR CoSTR PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576314)
- ILCOR 2020 strongly recommends establishing PAD (public access defibrillation) programs for OHCA; supporting evidence rated low certainty. — [AMC ILCOR Highlights](https://advancedmedicalcertification.com/highlights-of-the-2020-ilcor-guidelines-update)
- AHA data: layperson applied AED in only 11.9% of cases. — [AHA Part 3 Adult BLS](https://cpr.heart.org/en/test-cpr-pages/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines-2020/adult-basic-and-advanced-life-support)

### Inferences
- AHA 2020 and ERC 2021 are substantially aligned; the Sanjeevani app can use the same compression parameters for both.
- ILCOR is the evidence base; AHA and ERC translate it into regional guidelines. Training content grounded in ILCOR CoSTR satisfies both.
- The AHA's 2025 update (reflected in the Knowledge Booster app as of May 2026) supersedes 2020 for any areas where guidelines changed; the app should note its guideline version.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full AHA 2020 guideline PDF text (403 Forbidden); specific wording on dispatcher-assisted CPR and compression-only CPR for untrained bystanders was not confirmed from primary source.
- ERC 2025 guideline details (reportedly published) were not retrieved; 2021 is the current confirmed version used here.

## Indian Red Cross / Government First-Aid Guidelines

### Takeaway
India's primary lay first-aid framework is a 2015 evidence-based guideline developed jointly by the Indian Red Cross Society and Belgian Red Cross-Flanders, following AGREE II principles; these align broadly with international guidelines but were designed for India's resource context. Emergency number is 112 (national ERSS) with multiple activation methods.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence-based first-aid and prevention guidelines for lay people in India were developed by the Indian Red Cross Society and Belgian Red Cross-Flanders in 2015, using AGREE II methodology and PRISMA-reported systematic reviews. Published in Indian Journal of Community Health, Vol. 27, Issue 2 (2015), pp. 176-190. — [IJCH Journal](https://iapsmupuk.org/journal/index.php/IJCH/article/view/555); [Belgian Red Cross PDF](https://www.rodekruis.be/storage/main/pdf-10.pdf)
- The 112 ERSS (Emergency Response Support System) is India's pan-India single emergency number. Activation methods: dial 112; press power button 3 times quickly; use 112 India mobile app panic call; long-press '5' or '9' on a feature phone. — [112.gov.in](https://112.gov.in)
- The All India Institute of Local Self Government training material lists the aims of first aid as: preserve life, prevent worsening, promote recovery. It recommends elevation of bleeding limbs except when fracture is suspected. — [AIILSG Slideshow](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/first-aid-140911293/140911293)
- Indian Red Cross Society trained senior first-aid instructors through a joint project with the ICRC and Magen David Adom (Israel), enabling them to deliver updated first-aid curricula to civilians. — [Israel21C Archive](https://archive.israel21c.org/?p=82664)

### Inferences
- No official nationally-mandated Indian first-aid curriculum separate from the Indian Red Cross 2015 guidelines was found; the 2015 guidelines are the most authoritative Indian lay first-aid reference.
- Indian guidelines align with international best practice but do not update automatically; the app should note when content derives from international (AHA/ERC/ILCOR) rather than Indian Red Cross sources.
- The app must use 112 (not 911 or other numbers) in all call-to-action scenarios.

### Gaps
- No post-2015 update to the Indian Red Cross first-aid guidelines was found. Whether a 2020 or later revision exists could not be confirmed.
- No formal Indian government first-aid curriculum from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare was found in this search.

## Module-by-Module Analysis

### Module 1: CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

#### Takeaway
CPR is the highest-priority module given India's bystander CPR rate of 1.3-9.8% versus the AHA's 62% target. Digital simulation can teach the correct sequence and rate, but compression depth and quality cannot be assessed without physical feedback.

#### Cited Findings
- India's bystander CPR rate: 1.3% to 9.8% in most studies; AHA target is 62%. India's OHCA survival is under 10%. — [Indian Heart Journal 2023 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2c910debc389405c9b2ffecd86ed5a2c)
- A 2021 prospective study of 205 OHCA patients in India found only 9.8% received bystander CPR; AED was used in only 1%; ROSC in 8.3%; survival to discharge in 1.4%. — [PMC 8424282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8424282)
- Youth survey: 87% willing to assist an unconscious victim, 95% support CPR training in schools and colleges; but major gaps in CPR knowledge exist. — [PMC 8424282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8424282)
- AHA Knowledge Booster App explicitly states: the app does not replace a Heart Association course or produce an assigned eCard. It is a refresher activity. — [AHA Knowledge Booster FAQ](https://cpr.heart.org/en/-/media/CPR-Files/Courses-and-Kits/Healthcare-Professional/AHA-Knowledge-Booster-App-FAQ--Updated-as-of-052126-LOCKED.pdf)

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Recognize unresponsiveness and absence of normal breathing
2. Activate emergency services (112)
3. Perform chest compressions at correct rate (100-120/min) and approximate depth (5-6 cm / 2-2.4 in)
4. Deliver rescue breaths in 30:2 ratio (if trained)
5. Continue until AED arrives, EMS arrives, or victim recovers

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT delay compressions to find a pulse (lay persons should not check carotid pulse)
- Do NOT interrupt compressions for more than 10 seconds
- Call 112 before or immediately after starting CPR
- Compression rate must be 100-120/min (not faster, not slower)
- Hand placement: center of chest (lower half of sternum)

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Forgetting to call emergency services (112) first
- Wrong compression rate (app can simulate metronome feedback)
- Skipping the responsiveness check
- Omitting the head-tilt chin-lift airway opening step
- Incorrect 30:2 ratio
- Not continuing CPR until AED/EMS arrives

**Required Physical Assessment (cannot be simulated):**
- Actual compression depth (requires force-sensing mannequin or haptic device)
- Chest recoil (requires mannequin)
- Correct hand placement on sternum
- Rescue breath volume (requires mannequin with lung feedback)

**Medical Content Source:** AHA 2020 Guidelines Part 3; ILCOR CoSTR 2020 BLS Task Force

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- Emergency number is 112, not 911; app must be hardcoded to 112
- Bystander willingness is high; knowledge gap is the primary barrier
- Many bystanders may hesitate due to lack of confidence; app should emphasize compression-only CPR as acceptable for untrained rescuers

### Module 2: AED (Automated External Defibrillator)

#### Takeaway
AED use by laypersons is strongly recommended by ILCOR/AHA, but India has extremely low AED availability in public spaces; the training module has high educational value but should include messaging about AED rarity in India.

#### Cited Findings
- ILCOR 2020: strong recommendation for PAD programs; layperson AED use in OHCA was 11.9% in the US. — [AMC ILCOR Highlights](https://advancedmedicalcertification.com/highlights-of-the-2020-ilcor-guidelines-update)
- AED was used in only 1% of Indian OHCA patients in one study. — [PMC 8424282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8424282)
- Early electrical defibrillation before EMS arrival improves OHCA survival. — [Actabiomedica AED Study](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/9589)

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Recognize an AED and its components
2. Power on the AED correctly
3. Attach pads in correct positions (right clavicle, left side of chest)
4. Clear bystanders during analysis and shock delivery
5. Resume CPR immediately after shock

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Clear all bystanders before pressing shock button (do NOT touch the patient)
- Resume CPR immediately after shock — do not wait to check pulse
- Do NOT use AED on infants under 1 year unless pediatric pads/mode available
- Do NOT delay shock to continue CPR if AED is ready

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Pressing shock without clearing bystanders
- Failing to resume CPR after shock
- Incorrect pad placement
- Removing AED to continue elsewhere without giving shock when indicated

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Physical pad placement and adherence
- Actual button press force and timing
- Managing a real AED interface

**Medical Content Source:** ILCOR CoSTR 2020; AHA 2020 Part 3; ERC 2021

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- AED availability in India is very limited; module should include where to find an AED (airports, large hospitals) and note to call 112 even if no AED is nearby
- App should teach that any modern AED will give voice instructions; user does not need to memorize all steps

### Module 3: Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis

#### Takeaway
Anaphylaxis recognition and epinephrine auto-injector (EAI) training is safety-critical; epinephrine is the only life-saving first-line treatment, and delays cause deaths. The app must clearly distinguish mild allergic reactions from anaphylaxis without implying diagnostic authority.

#### Cited Findings
- Adult epinephrine auto-injector dose: 0.3 mg intramuscularly, outer upper thigh; second dose after 15 minutes if reaction continues. — [UCOP Epinephrine Emergency Plan](https://www.ucop.edu/safety-and-loss-prevention/_files/field-research-safety/epinephrine-emergency-action-plan.pdf)
- A 2017 review found inadequate EAI training among the gaps in anaphylaxis management; correct prescription and training of health professionals needs monitoring. — [PubMed 28888248](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28888248/)
- Training programs for EAI use must include recognizing severe allergic reactions, storage and administration, and post-administration emergency medical follow-up. — [DC Council Code](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/7-2381.04)
- AHA 2020 first aid section includes anaphylaxis. Epinephrine auto-injectors are recommended as the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis by WAO and EAACI guidelines.

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Recognize signs of anaphylaxis (hives + breathing difficulty + low blood pressure / dizziness = systemic reaction)
2. Distinguish mild allergic reaction (hives, itching only) from anaphylaxis (breathing difficulty, swelling of throat/tongue, shock)
3. Call 112 immediately for suspected anaphylaxis
4. Assist person to use their prescribed EAI (or use prescribed EAI on them) — outer thigh, through clothing if needed
5. Position person: sitting upright if breathing difficulty; lying down with legs elevated if dizzy/faint; recovery position if unconscious
6. Administer second dose after 15 minutes if no improvement and EMS not arrived
7. Be prepared to perform CPR if patient loses consciousness

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT give epinephrine unless signs of systemic reaction are present (not just hives alone)
- Call 112 even after giving epinephrine — epinephrine is a bridge, not a cure
- Do NOT have patient stand or walk; lay them flat with legs elevated unless they are vomiting or have breathing difficulty
- Do NOT administer oral antihistamines as substitute for epinephrine in anaphylaxis

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Failing to call 112 after epinephrine
- Choosing antihistamine over epinephrine
- Not positioning patient correctly
- Missing the systemic reaction indicators and dismissing it as mild

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Physical EAI technique (needle insertion, pressure, hold time)
- Actual injection site locating on outer thigh

**Medical Content Source:** AHA 2020 First Aid; WAO Anaphylaxis Guidelines; EAACI

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- Epinephrine auto-injectors have limited availability in India; app should note that 0.3 mg epinephrine IM injection by any route is acceptable
- App must frame as "recognition aid and action guide" — not diagnostic; must not claim to diagnose anaphylaxis
- Always directs to call 112; never implies self-management is sufficient

### Module 4: Stroke FAST Recognition

#### Takeaway
FAST (Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call) is validated; at least one of three FAST signs is present in 88% of strokes and TIAs. The app must be framed exclusively as a recognition and activation tool, never as a diagnostic tool.

#### Cited Findings
- One or more of face weakness, arm weakness, and speech difficulty are present in 88% of all strokes and TIAs. The FAST acronym is included in AHA/ASA public education materials. — [AHA Ischemic Stroke Guideline via eGuideline](https://eguideline.guidelinecentral.com/i/123200/1)
- A smartphone-based FAST app showed significant gains in knowledge and behavior for early stroke recognition in an Indonesian mixed-methods study. — [Unnes Journal](https://journal.unnes.ac.id/journals/kemas/article/download/38954/11508/193194)
- Intense and ongoing public education about stroke signs and symptoms improves stroke recognition rates. AHA data: fewer than half of 911 calls for stroke were made within 1 hour of onset. — [AHA Ischemic Stroke Guideline via eGuideline](https://eguideline.guidelinecentral.com/i/123200/1)
- FAST.AI (machine learning app) analyzed facial video, arm sensors, and voice; cited as a "validated approach" for stroke sign detection at ASA conference. This is a detection tool, distinct from a training tool. — [Neuronews](https://neuronewsinternational.com/smartphone-app-identify-stroke-symptoms-isc-2023/)

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Identify the FAST acronym: Face, Arms, Speech, Time
2. Conduct a FAST assessment on a simulated patient
3. Recognize when even one positive FAST sign warrants calling 112
4. Understand time-sensitivity: stroke treatment window (thrombolytics within 4.5 hours)
5. Note the time of symptom onset
6. Do NOT give food or water
7. Keep patient calm and lying down

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Call 112 immediately on ANY positive FAST sign — do not wait for multiple signs
- Note exact time symptoms were first observed
- Do NOT give aspirin unless directed by EMS/physician (contraindicated in hemorrhagic stroke)
- Do NOT give food or water (aspiration risk)

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Waiting for all three FAST signs before calling 112
- Not noting time of symptom onset
- Giving aspirin or food/water
- Dismissing single positive sign as minor

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- None — all FAST steps are observable / callable from a simulation

**Medical Content Source:** AHA/ASA Acute Ischemic Stroke Guidelines; FAST campaign evidence

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- App must display clear disclaimer: "This is a recognition aid only. Only a physician can diagnose stroke."
- Stroke is a leading cause of disability in India; the app should note the extreme importance of calling 112 fast
- Risk of content being misused: patient or family may delay 112 call to complete the FAST assessment; app should stress speed

### Module 5: Choking / Heimlich Maneuver

#### Takeaway
Adult choking first aid uses a recognized alternating back-blow and abdominal-thrust sequence; the app can simulate the decision tree but cannot assess physical technique adequacy.

#### Cited Findings
- For adult choking: ask "Are you choking?"; if they cannot speak or cough, begin intervention. Steps: 5 back blows between shoulder blades (heel of hand), then 5 abdominal thrusts (fist above navel, cover with other hand, pull inward and upward). Alternate until object cleared or person becomes unresponsive. — [ECU Health Library](https://healthlibrary.ecuhealth.org/emergency-and-urgent-care/treatment/first-aid-choking-adult-or-child); [Vanderbilt Health](https://healthlibrary.vanderbilthealth.com/Library/HealthSheets/3,S,83412)
- If unconscious: call 112, begin CPR; each time airway is opened to give breaths, look for an object. — Multiple hospital health libraries (consistent across sources)
- For pregnancy/obesity: use chest thrusts against middle of breastbone instead of abdominal thrusts. — Multiple hospital health library sources

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Distinguish mild (effective cough) from severe (no cough or sound) choking
2. Do not intervene for mild choking — let person cough
3. For severe choking: perform 5 back blows then 5 abdominal thrusts; alternate
4. Manage special cases: pregnant/obese — chest thrusts instead
5. If person becomes unresponsive: call 112, begin CPR sequence

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT intervene if person is coughing effectively (may worsen situation)
- Correctly locate navel for fist placement (above navel, below sternum)
- If unconscious: immediately transition to CPR protocol; do not continue thrusts
- Chest thrusts for pregnancy/obesity — not abdominal

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Intervening when person is still coughing effectively
- Giving thrusts to unconscious person without transitioning to CPR
- Wrong decision tree (abdominal vs chest thrusts)
- Not calling 112

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Actual thrust depth and direction
- Physical back blow force and location
- Body positioning relative to victim

**Medical Content Source:** AHA First Aid Guidelines; Indian Red Cross 2015 guidelines

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- No India-specific deviation from international guidelines found
- Cultural note: potential reluctance to perform back blows on elders; app should normalize the procedure

### Module 6: Recovery Position

#### Takeaway
Recovery position is a simple, evidence-based intervention for unconscious breathing patients; it prevents aspiration and is easily teachable digitally.

#### Cited Findings
- Recovery position is taught in AHA BLS, ERC 2021, and Indian Red Cross materials as the appropriate management of an unconscious breathing victim.
- Indian Red Cross 2015 guidelines include recovery position as a core first-aid skill.

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Assess: unconscious but breathing normally
2. Call 112
3. Roll victim onto side (left lateral position), stabilize with bent knee
4. Ensure airway is open (head tilted back slightly)
5. Monitor breathing and be prepared to start CPR if breathing stops

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT use recovery position if patient has suspected spinal injury from trauma
- Recheck breathing every 2 minutes
- Call 112 before or immediately upon discovery of unconsciousness

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Using recovery position for unresponsive non-breathing patient (should do CPR instead)
- Not calling 112
- Applying to trauma patient with neck injury risk

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Physical body rolling technique
- Stabilizing leg position

**Medical Content Source:** AHA 2020 First Aid; ERC 2021; Indian Red Cross 2015

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- No significant deviation from international guidelines

### Module 7: Severe Bleeding

#### Takeaway
Direct pressure is the most effective and teachable intervention for severe external bleeding; tourniquet use as a last resort is teachable and important in a road-accident context (India has among the world's highest road traffic fatality rates).

#### Cited Findings
- Direct pressure is the primary intervention: apply firm pressure with clean cloth/dressing for at least 15 minutes; do not lift pad; add layers on top if blood soaks through. — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/emergencies-and-first-aid-direct-pressure-to-stop-bleeding); [First Aid Pro Australia](https://www.firstaidpro.com.au/blog/first-aid-for-severe-bleeding/)
- Elevation helps: raise the bleeding limb above heart level if possible. AIILSG Indian guidance notes not to elevate if fracture is suspected. — [AIILSG via Slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/first-aid-140911293/140911293)
- Do not remove embedded objects; pack around them. — MedlinePlus; multiple sources
- Tourniquet: last resort for limb bleeding; place 2-3 inches above wound, not over joint; note time of application. — [Uncharted Supply Co](https://unchartedsupplyco.com/blogs/the-four-pillars/how-to-stop-severe-bleeding)
- Watch for shock signs: pale/cold/clammy skin; lay person down. — Multiple sources

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Identify life-threatening bleeding (pooling blood, soaking clothing quickly, spurting)
2. Call 112
3. Apply direct pressure with clean cloth for 15 minutes
4. Add layers; do not remove blood-soaked dressing
5. Elevate limb (unless fracture suspected)
6. For embedded objects: do not remove; pack around and apply pressure
7. Apply tourniquet for uncontrolled limb bleed (last resort)
8. Recognize shock; lay patient flat; keep warm

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT remove an embedded object from a wound
- Do NOT lift the pressure pad to check the wound
- Call 112 for any serious wound (not just after steps are taken)
- Tourniquet: note time of application

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Removing embedded object
- Lifting pressure pad to check
- Not calling 112
- Failing to add layers when blood soaks through
- Applying tourniquet directly over joint

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Actual pressure applied (force)
- Correct tourniquet tightening

**Medical Content Source:** AHA 2020 First Aid; Indian Red Cross 2015; AIILSG guidelines

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- India's road traffic accident fatality rate is among the highest globally; severe bleeding from trauma is a major emergency
- 108 is India's dedicated ambulance number (also reachable via 112 ERSS dispatch); app should mention 112 as single number

### Module 8: Seizure Management

#### Takeaway
Seizure first aid is primarily protective and observational; the most common dangerous errors are restraining the person and putting objects in their mouth. Both are easily trained and error-detectable digitally.

#### Cited Findings
- Do NOT restrain a person having a seizure. — [Epilepsy Ireland PDF](https://www.epilepsy.ie/sites/www.epilepsy.ie/files/Convulsive%20seizure%20first%20aid.pdf); [Epilepsy Foundation Feb 2020](https://earlychildhood.marylandpublicschools.org/system/files/filedepot/3/seizurefirstaid_-epilepsy_foundation_1.pdf)
- Do NOT put anything in the person's mouth. — Epilepsy Ireland; Epilepsy Foundation
- Time the seizure. Call emergency services if seizure lasts > 5 minutes, repeated seizures occur, first-ever seizure, injury, or difficulty breathing. — [Epilepsy Foundation 2020](https://earlychildhood.marylandpublicschools.org/system/files/filedepot/3/seizurefirstaid_-epilepsy_foundation_1.pdf); [University of Florida Emergency](https://emergency.ufl.edu/take-action/medical-emergency-seizures/)
- Stay with person; protect head with soft cushioning; clear area of sharp objects. Roll onto side after convulsions stop (recovery position). — Multiple sources
- For non-convulsive seizures: gently guide person away from danger. — Multiple sources
- Rescue medications should only be given if prescribed by a healthcare professional. — Multiple sources

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Recognize a seizure (convulsive vs. absence/non-convulsive)
2. Do not restrain; do not put anything in mouth
3. Protect head; clear dangerous objects; time the seizure
4. Call 112 if seizure > 5 min, repeated, first-time, injury, or breathing difficulty
5. After seizure: roll to recovery position; reassure; stay until fully alert

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Never restrain the person
- Never put anything in the mouth (including fingers, wallet, etc.)
- Call 112 if seizure > 5 minutes
- Do NOT give water/food until person is fully alert

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Choosing to restrain the person
- Choosing to put something in the mouth
- Not timing the seizure
- Not calling 112 when duration > 5 minutes
- Not transitioning to recovery position post-seizure

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- None — all seizure first aid steps are observational/positional and fully simulatable

**Medical Content Source:** Epilepsy Foundation 2020; AHA 2020 First Aid; Indian Red Cross 2015

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- Cultural myths around seizures are common in India (e.g., belief that objects in the mouth prevent swallowing the tongue); app should explicitly counter this
- Epilepsy stigma in India may cause bystanders to flee; app should normalize appropriate response

### Module 9: Burns First Aid

#### Takeaway
The evidence-based burn cooling protocol (cool running water for 20 minutes within 3 hours) is robustly supported and directly teachable; common errors (using ice, applying butter/toothpaste) are major harm risks and easily trained against.

#### Cited Findings
- Cool with running water for 20 minutes; effective if started within 3 hours of injury. Associated with decreased burn depth and significantly lower odds of needing a skin graft. — [Griffith University 2020 Annals of Emergency Medicine study](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/98258500-cf2b-4284-b091-ad99dabae7d0/content)
- Keep cool, call, cover: remove rings/clothing; use cling film loosely (not on face). — [QVH NHS](https://www.qvh.nhs.uk/?p=804)
- Do NOT use ice (causes frostbite/vasoconstriction). — [MayoClinic Burns](https://www.mayoclinic.org/first-aid/FA00022); multiple sources
- Do NOT apply butter, egg white, toothpaste, or other home remedies. — Multiple sources
- Cool burn but keep person warm (prevent hypothermia, especially in children). — Multiple sources
- 71.9% of children in one Australian study received adequate first aid; only 33.1% received it at the scene. — [Griffith University](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/98258500-cf2b-4284-b091-ad99dabae7d0/content)
- Seek urgent care for burns on face/hands/feet/joints/genitals, chemical or electrical burns, or burns larger than the victim's palm. — MayoClinic; NHS

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Stop the burning (move away, stop-drop-roll)
2. Cool with running water for 20 minutes
3. Remove rings/clothing near burn (not if stuck)
4. Cover loosely with cling film or clean non-fluffy cloth
5. Keep person warm (prevent hypothermia)
6. Call 112 for serious burns; know criteria for seeking emergency care

**Safety-Critical Steps (must be deterministic):**
- Do NOT use ice — causes additional injury
- Do NOT apply butter, toothpaste, or other substances
- Do NOT remove melted clothing stuck to skin
- Cool for full 20 minutes — not just a brief rinse
- Call 112 for serious burns before completing all steps

**Detectable Errors (via digital simulation):**
- Choosing ice over cool water
- Choosing to apply butter/toothpaste
- Cooling for only a brief period
- Removing stuck clothing/jewelry
- Not calling 112 for serious burns

**Required Physical Assessment:**
- Actual water temperature assessment (moderate coolness)
- Judging burn severity by visual inspection

**Medical Content Source:** AHA 2020 First Aid; WHO First Aid Guidelines; ERC 2021 First Aid section

**India-Specific Considerations:**
- Butter/oil application to burns is a prevalent traditional practice in India; the app must specifically counter this
- Fire burns from kerosene stoves and cooking fires are common in Indian homes; this module has high public-health relevance
- Running water may not be immediately available in some Indian settings; tepid water from any clean source is acceptable

## Regulatory Considerations for India

### Takeaway
A pure training app with no diagnostic/monitoring/treatment intent likely falls outside CDSCO's medical device software (MDSW) regulation; however, the intended-use statement is critical and a wellness label alone does not avoid regulation if diagnostic claims are implied.

### Cited Findings
- CDSCO finalized guidance (CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026) on medical device software under MDR 2017. The scope covers any software intended for a medical purpose, including SaMD, SiMD, AI-enabled medical software, and some mobile apps. — [Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/india-cdsco-finalizes-guidance-medical-device-software); [India Briefing](https://india-briefing.com/news/cdsco-draft-guidance-medical-software-40691.html)
- Excluded from MDSW scope: general wellness apps, quality system or ERP software, data management software without medical purpose. A wellness label alone does not neutralize diagnostic, monitoring, prediction, treatment, or other medical claims. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance)
- Software uses the same four risk classes as hardware under MDR 2017 (Class A through D). Intended use is the key determiner. — [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/the-next-frontier-in-medtech-regulation-dissecting-cdscos-draft-guidance-on-medical-device-software-15465)
- No CDSCO source directly addressed training-only apps; inference only. — Multiple CDSCO coverage sources

### Inferences
- If Sanjeevani explicitly frames all content as training/education and includes no patient-specific diagnostic/monitoring claims, it likely falls outside MDSW regulation as a "general wellness app."
- The FAST module (stroke recognition) and anaphylaxis module are the highest regulatory-risk areas if the app language implies diagnosis. All such modules must state explicitly: "This is a recognition aid for lay responders. Only a healthcare professional can diagnose [condition]."
- Any future AI-based real-time assessment of patient symptoms (e.g., video analysis for FAST) would likely trigger MDSW classification.

### Gaps
- No direct CDSCO ruling on first-aid training apps was found. Formal regulatory guidance should be sought from CDSCO or a qualified Indian regulatory consultant before app launch.
- India's IT Act and Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023 may impose additional requirements for health data collection; not addressed here.

## Disclaimers Required / Recommended

### Takeaway
AHA and major CPR training organizations require disclaimers that training apps do not confer certification, do not guarantee proficiency, and do not substitute for hands-on training. Apps must prominently direct users to call 112 in real emergencies.

### Cited Findings
- AHA Knowledge Booster App: "Using the AHA Knowledge Booster App does not replace a Heart Association course or produce an assigned eCard. It functions as a refresher activity to enhance your education retention over time." — [AHA Knowledge Booster FAQ](https://cpr.heart.org/en/-/media/CPR-Files/Courses-and-Kits/Healthcare-Professional/AHA-Knowledge-Booster-App-FAQ--Updated-as-of-052126-LOCKED.pdf)

## Training vs. Real Emergency Boundary

### Takeaway
The boundary between simulation and real emergency must be explicit throughout the app; any ambiguity creates liability and potential harm.

### Cited Findings
- AHA explicitly positions its training app as a refresher, not a real-emergency reference. — [AHA Knowledge Booster FAQ](https://cpr.heart.org/en/-/media/CPR-Files/Courses-and-Kits/Healthcare-Professional/AHA-Knowledge-Booster-App-FAQ--Updated-as-of-052126-LOCKED.pdf)
- 112 ERSS allows activation via mobile app panic call; the training app should never expose a call-112 button that could accidentally activate a real emergency call. — [112.gov.in](https://112.gov.in)

### Inferences
- The app must use a clear visual distinction (e.g., "TRAINING MODE" banner) during all simulation scenarios.
- Any in-app "Call 112" UI must either: (a) be disabled in simulation mode and enabled only in a real emergency handoff screen, or (b) be clearly labeled "Exit Training - Call 112 for Real Emergency."
- The app should have an emergency exit accessible from any screen that breaks simulation and provides real emergency guidance.
- If user is mid-simulation and reports "real emergency occurring now," the app should immediately display: "Exit training. Call 112 now."

## Summary of India-Specific Deviations from Western Guidelines

| Topic | Western Standard | India Consideration |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Emergency number | 911 (US), 999 (UK) | 112 (ERSS) — all app CTAs must use 112 |
| CPR training infrastructure | Wide access to mannequin-based courses | Limited; app fills critical gap |
| AED availability | Growing public access programs | Very limited; training value is future-oriented |
| Burns treatment | Do not apply home remedies | Butter/oil application is culturally prevalent; must be explicitly countered |
| Seizure response | Standard international guidance | Widespread myths (objects in mouth); must be explicitly countered |
| Anaphylaxis | Epinephrine auto-injectors widely available | EAI availability limited; IM injection guidance more relevant |
| Good Samaritan protection | Varies by jurisdiction | India has protections for road accident bystanders since 2019 |
| First aid guidelines | AHA 2020, ERC 2021, ILCOR 2020 | Indian Red Cross 2015 guidelines (last confirmed update); broadly aligned with international standards |
